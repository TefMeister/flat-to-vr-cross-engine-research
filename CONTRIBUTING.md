# Contributing

Anyone is welcome to add to this library. It exists so the public knowledge on modding flat
games into VR lives in one readable, well-attributed place. Contributions are held to three
simple, non-negotiable rules.

## The three rules

1. **Public information only — and read it online, not downloaded.** Everything you add must
   already be publicly available — a public repo, a docs site, a forum/Discord/Reddit post, an
   article, a video, a released tool. Research it strictly online (browser, web fetch, a repo's own
   file viewer) — never clone or download someone else's repo, mod, or files locally just to study
   them. Do not add private communications, leaked material, or anything behind an NDA. Do not add
   copyrighted engine source or game assets.

   **Your own original research counts as public once you publish it.** If you did the work
   yourself — on a game you legitimately own — you may contribute your findings here, provided you
   publish the underlying evidence somewhere public and link it, and label the entry as your own
   first-party work rather than a summary of someone else's. Findings about an engine are fine;
   game assets, extracted content, and bulk dumps are not.
2. **Link the primary source; never paste their code.** Say where each fact, technique, or figure
   comes from and link the exact repo/commit/doc. Describe mechanisms **entirely in your own
   words**. Do **not** paste, transcribe, or lightly-reword anyone else's source code, config, or
   files into this library — **not even a short snippet, regardless of license or price.** If code
   would help, write your **own** minimal illustration from scratch (or generic pseudocode) and
   label it as ours; otherwise just link to theirs. You study everything public freely, online; what
   lands in this repo is only ever our own words and our own code.
3. **Credit everyone.** Add each new person, project, or tool you drew on to
   [`ATTRIBUTION.md`](./ATTRIBUTION.md), with a link and — where relevant — their license. If in
   doubt, over-credit.

## What belongs here

- Engine-agnostic techniques and patterns (hooks, stereo, timing, projection, HUD, input).
- Worked case studies of engines someone has already brought into VR **in public**, described
  as study notes with links — not copied source trees.
- Landscape/orientation material: which public tool fits which engine, and why.
- Corrections and updates to anything already here.

## What does not belong here

- Any of another project's source code or files — whole **or** partial, verbatim **or** reworded.
  Link to them and describe the idea in your own words instead.
- Game assets, copyrighted engine source, or any non-public material.
- Circumvention guidance aimed at DRM/anti-cheat for its own sake — this library documents
  rendering/VR techniques for legitimately-owned games, not piracy or cheating.

## How to contribute

Open a pull request or an issue. Keep docs in Markdown, match the existing structure, and update
`ATTRIBUTION.md` in the same PR. By contributing you agree your original writing is licensed
under this repo's [CC-BY-4.0](./LICENSE); any third-party material you reference stays under its
own license.

## Our side of the deal

We hold other people's work to **link-and-learn, never copy** — but our own work is the opposite:
**everything *we* create — the writing in this library, our tooling, and our mods — is free for
anyone to use, modify, and build on, as long as you credit us.** The writing here is
[CC-BY-4.0](./LICENSE); take it freely, just say where it came from. We built every bit of it
ourselves by studying what's public and doing our own trial and error, and we're glad for others
to stand on it the same way.

## Corrections & removals

If you are credited or referenced here — as an author, contributor, or source — and want your
name or information changed or removed, email **td3kxlvr@proton.me** and tell us **exactly what to
remove**. We will do it promptly, no problem and no argument; you do not need to prove ownership or
explain why. Likewise, if you should be credited and aren't, tell us and we'll fix it. And to be
clear: we document techniques and link to sources — **we do not copy or reuse anyone's actual code
or files**, regardless of license or price.
